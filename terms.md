## 2. What Miko is

Miko reads the ingredient list off a product you photograph, tells you what is
in it, and — if you choose to put products on a shelf — builds a weekly routine
around what commonly should not be layered.

**What it is not:** a diagnosis, a prescription, a substitute for a
dermatologist, or a guarantee about any product's safety, contents or effect.

## 6. AI-generated content, and its limits

Much of what Miko shows you is produced by an AI model reading a photograph and
your answers. You should understand three things about that:

1. **It can be wrong.** It can misread a label, miss an ingredient, or state
   something incorrect with confidence. Check anything that matters against the
   physical product.
2. **A recalled ingredient list may be out of date.** When the model recognises
   a product from its front, the ingredients come from what it knows about that
   product rather than from your photograph. Formulas change without packaging
   changing. The app marks these, and you should photograph the back to be sure.
3. **Our reviewed reference is a work in progress.** Miko distinguishes claims
   drawn from its own reference table from the model's own reading. As of this
   date **none of the reference entries have completed citation review**, and
   the app labels them "Pending review" and caps their stated confidence
   accordingly. Do not treat any claim in the app as a sourced medical fact.

## Notes for whoever finalises this

1. **§5 is the clause this app lives or dies on.** It is written to be
   protective and to be honest, but it is the one section a lawyer must see. The
   pregnancy feature is simultaneously the highest-value thing in the product
   and its only real liability surface.
2. **§6.3 will need editing the day the citation review completes.** It
   currently states that no reference entry has been reviewed, which is true and
   matches what the app displays. Leaving it in once it is false would be worse
   than never having written it.
3. **§3 must match App Store Connect exactly** — product names, prices, periods.
   The paywall now reads its prices from the RevenueCat offering
   (`pricesFromOffering` in `src/purchases/map.ts`), so what a customer sees is
   the storefront's own number. This table is the last place a price is written
   by hand, which makes it the one that can drift from what is charged.
4. **No trial, and no free tier.** Both are deliberate
   (docs/subscriptions.md §2), and together they mean the first thing a new user
   is asked to do is pay. The renewal terms therefore have to be unmissable on
   the paywall itself rather than only here — they are, in `styles.terms`. A
   weekly tier was considered and dropped for the scrutiny it attracts on
   low-engagement apps; monthly at US$9.99 raises none of that.
5. **Fill every `[BRACKET]`**, and pick the jurisdiction deliberately — India
   and the UK give different answers on consumer rights and on the liability cap
   in §10.
